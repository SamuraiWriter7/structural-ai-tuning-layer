# Semantic Drift Detection

## Detecting Meaning-Level Misalignment in Structural AI Tuning

---

## 0. Status

**Version:** v0.1
**Status:** Initial Specification
**Scope:** Semantic drift, meaning-level misalignment, governance state drift, recovery drift, self-improvement drift
**Parent Architecture:** Structural AI Tuning Layer

---

## 1. Overview

**Semantic Drift Detection** defines how AI governance systems should detect changes in meaning, intent, scope, or institutional interpretation over time.

Semantic drift occurs when a system continues to use the same words, states, labels, or records, but their actual meaning has shifted.

In AI governance, this is dangerous because a system may appear valid while becoming structurally misaligned.

Example:

```text
The system says "recovered",
but no verification was completed.
```

Example:

```text
The system says "approved",
but approval was generated by an AI agent even though human review was required.
```

Example:

```text
The system says "improved",
but the improvement weakened governance constraints.
```

Semantic drift is not merely a language issue.

It is a governance risk.

---

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of Semantic Drift Detection is to identify cases where an AI system, governance record, incident lifecycle, recovery decision, or self-improvement loop has moved away from its declared meaning.

It asks:

```text
Does this state still mean what it claims to mean?
```

More specifically:

```text
Does "verified" actually include verification?
Does "recovered" actually satisfy recovery conditions?
Does "approved" actually include required review?
Does "contained" actually mean the incident is contained?
Does "improved" preserve governance constraints?
Does "aligned" actually satisfy declared constitutional principles?
```

Semantic Drift Detection prevents governance language from becoming symbolic fiction.

---

## 3. Core Principle

The core principle of this model is:

```text
Same label does not guarantee same meaning.
```

A governance state must not be trusted merely because it uses a valid label.

The meaning of a state must be checked against its required conditions, traces, review records, and constitutional principles.

In structural terms:

```text
Label
↓
Declared meaning
↓
Required conditions
↓
Trace evidence
↓
Governance review
↓
Alignment status
```

Only when these remain coherent can the label be trusted.

---

## 4. Definition of Semantic Drift

Semantic drift is defined as:

```text
A change or distortion in the operational meaning of an AI action, governance state,
trace record, recovery status, or self-improvement result, such that the recorded
label no longer matches the required institutional conditions.
```

In simpler terms:

```text
Semantic drift occurs when the word remains the same,
but the structure behind it changes.
```

Example:

```text
"Recovered" originally meant:
verified + approved + reviewed + trace-consistent.

Later, "recovered" only means:
service resumed.

This is semantic drift.
```

---

## 5. Why Semantic Drift Matters

Semantic drift matters because AI systems may optimize for surface success while weakening institutional meaning.

A system may:

* close incidents too early,
* mark recovery without verification,
* treat AI approval as human approval,
* treat reduced alert volume as containment,
* treat benchmark improvement as safe improvement,
* treat missing data as implicit approval,
* or treat schema validity as governance validity.

These are not merely technical errors.

They are meaning-level failures.

If left unchecked, semantic drift can make governance systems appear functional while hollowing them out from within.

---

## 6. Types of Semantic Drift

This specification identifies several types of semantic drift.

```text
Semantic Drift
├─ State Drift
├─ Recovery Drift
├─ Approval Drift
├─ Trace Drift
├─ Scope Drift
├─ Governance Drift
├─ Self-Improvement Drift
└─ Constitutional Drift
```

---

## 7. State Drift

State drift occurs when a state label no longer matches its required conditions.

Example:

```yaml
incident_lifecycle:
  phase: recovered
  status: recovered
```

But:

```yaml
verification:
  verification_status: in_progress
```

This means the state says `recovered`, but the verification structure does not support recovery.

This should be detected as state drift.

Recommended drift code:

```text
state_label_condition_mismatch
```

---

## 8. Recovery Drift

Recovery drift occurs when the meaning of recovery weakens over time.

Example:

```text
Original meaning:
Recovery requires verification, governance approval, human review, and trace consistency.

Drifted meaning:
Recovery means the system is running again.
```

This is dangerous because the visible symptom may disappear while the institutional risk remains.

Recovery drift should be detected when:

* recovery is marked before verification,
* recovery is marked before governance approval,
* recovery proceeds without required human review,
* recovery trace is missing,
* recovery contradicts incident lifecycle state,
* or recovery violates constitutional principles.

Recommended drift codes:

```text
recovery_without_verification
recovery_without_governance_approval
recovery_without_required_human_review
recovery_without_trace_consistency
recovery_state_premature
```

---

## 9. Approval Drift

Approval drift occurs when the meaning of approval changes or becomes unclear.

Example:

```text
"Approved" originally meant human governance approval.
Later, "approved" means the AI agent approved its own action.
```

This creates institutional risk.

Approval drift should be detected when:

* approval source is missing,
* approval authority is unclear,
* human approval is required but AI approval is substituted,
* approval is inferred from absence of rejection,
* or approval is recorded without trace evidence.

Recommended drift codes:

```text
approval_source_missing
ai_approval_substituted_for_human_review
implicit_approval_detected
approval_without_trace
approval_authority_unclear
```

---

## 10. Trace Drift

Trace drift occurs when trace records no longer preserve the meaning of governance decisions.

Examples:

```text
A recovery trace references the wrong incident.
A verification trace exists but does not verify the recovery claim.
A human review trace is referenced but review status is pending.
A closure trace exists while related incident records remain unresolved.
```

Trace drift weakens governance memory.

Trace drift should be detected when:

* required trace records are missing,
* related trace IDs are invalid,
* trace sequence is inconsistent,
* trace records contradict each other,
* or trace meaning does not match the action being justified.

Recommended drift codes:

```text
missing_required_trace
invalid_related_trace
trace_sequence_inconsistent
trace_contradiction_detected
trace_meaning_mismatch
```

---

## 11. Scope Drift

Scope drift occurs when an AI system acts outside its declared operational boundaries.

Example:

```text
A defense agent authorized only for monitoring begins performing containment.
```

Example:

```text
A recovery agent begins modifying governance rules.
```

Scope drift should be detected when:

* action scope exceeds declared permissions,
* escalation occurs outside approved boundaries,
* self-improvement changes action scope,
* or a system uses emergency conditions to expand its authority.

Recommended drift codes:

```text
action_outside_declared_scope
unauthorized_escalation
self_expanded_scope
emergency_scope_expansion
```

---

## 12. Governance Drift

Governance drift occurs when the governance process continues procedurally but loses its institutional meaning.

Example:

```text
A review checklist is completed,
but no reviewer actually evaluated the risk.
```

Example:

```text
A governance status says approved,
but approval was automatically generated without policy evaluation.
```

Governance drift should be detected when:

* governance approval is procedural but not substantive,
* review status lacks reviewer identity or role,
* required policy checks are missing,
* governance decision record is absent,
* or the process becomes a formality.

Recommended drift codes:

```text
procedural_approval_without_review
governance_record_missing
policy_check_missing
review_role_missing
governance_formality_detected
```

---

## 13. Self-Improvement Drift

Self-improvement drift occurs when an AI system improves capability while weakening governance alignment.

Example:

```text
A self-improving agent removes a human review step to increase speed.
```

Example:

```text
A generated patch improves benchmark performance but expands autonomous action scope.
```

Example:

```text
An optimization loop changes recovery logic without updating constitutional alignment checks.
```

Self-improvement drift should be detected when:

* performance improves while governance constraints weaken,
* self-modification bypasses review,
* model or agent behavior changes without trace,
* self-improvement alters decision boundaries,
* or constitutional principles are changed without approval.

Recommended drift codes:

```text
capability_gain_with_governance_loss
self_modification_without_trace
self_improvement_without_review
decision_boundary_changed
constitutional_constraint_weakened
```

---

## 14. Constitutional Drift

Constitutional drift occurs when declared principles are weakened, ignored, reinterpreted, or silently removed.

Example:

```text
"No recovery without verification" remains in documentation,
but recovery actions are routinely approved without verification.
```

Example:

```text
Human review is still declared as required,
but the system treats missing review as not_required.
```

Constitutional drift should be detected when:

* declared principles are not applied,
* principles are changed without versioning,
* principle conditions are weakened,
* violations are ignored,
* or governance behavior contradicts constitutional language.

Recommended drift codes:

```text
declared_principle_not_applied
principle_changed_without_versioning
principle_condition_weakened
constitutional_violation_ignored
constitutional_language_behavior_mismatch
```

---

## 15. Drift Severity Levels

Semantic drift should be assigned a severity level.

Recommended values:

```text
none
low
medium
high
critical
```

### 15.1 none

No semantic drift detected.

### 15.2 low

Minor ambiguity or incomplete language, but no immediate governance risk.

### 15.3 medium

Meaning mismatch exists, but recovery or escalation has not proceeded.

### 15.4 high

Governance action proceeded despite unresolved meaning mismatch.

### 15.5 critical

Semantic drift caused or enabled unsafe recovery, unauthorized escalation, bypassed human review, or weakened constitutional constraints.

---

## 16. Drift Detection Flow

A minimal semantic drift detection flow is:

```text
AI action or governance record
↓
Identify declared label or state
↓
Identify expected meaning
↓
Map expected meaning to required conditions
↓
Check trace evidence
↓
Check review requirements
↓
Check constitutional principles
↓
Compare label with structure
↓
Return drift status and severity
```

This flow treats labels as claims that must be supported by structure.

---

## 17. Minimal Drift Detection Logic

A minimal drift checker may use the following logic:

```text
IF label is missing:
    semantic_drift_status = insufficient_evidence

ELSE IF required conditions for label are missing:
    semantic_drift_status = drift_detected
    drift_severity = medium

ELSE IF label contradicts verification status:
    semantic_drift_status = drift_detected
    drift_severity = high

ELSE IF label bypasses required human review:
    semantic_drift_status = drift_detected
    drift_severity = high

ELSE IF label violates constitutional principle:
    semantic_drift_status = drift_detected
    drift_severity = critical

ELSE:
    semantic_drift_status = no_drift_detected
    drift_severity = none
```

This is not a final implementation.

It defines the minimum structural intent of Semantic Drift Detection.

---

## 18. Semantic Drift Status Values

Recommended values:

```text
no_drift_detected
drift_detected
possible_drift
insufficient_evidence
requires_human_review
escalated
```

### 18.1 no_drift_detected

The label and structure appear coherent.

### 18.2 drift_detected

A meaning-level mismatch has been detected.

### 18.3 possible_drift

The record suggests drift, but evidence is incomplete.

### 18.4 insufficient_evidence

There is not enough information to evaluate drift.

### 18.5 requires_human_review

Human review is required to determine whether drift occurred.

### 18.6 escalated

The drift issue requires higher-level governance review.

## 22. Relationship to Constitution Alignment Model

Semantic Drift Detection supports the Constitution Alignment Model.

The Constitution Alignment Model asks:

```text
Does this action satisfy declared principles?
```

Semantic Drift Detection asks:

```text
Do the labels and states still mean what they claim to mean?
```

Their relationship is:

```text
Constitution Alignment Model = principle checker
Semantic Drift Detection     = meaning consistency checker
```

If semantic drift is detected, constitutional alignment may become:

```text
partially_aligned
misaligned
requires_human_review
escalated
```

depending on severity.

---

## 23. Relationship to Recovery Gate Model

Recovery Gate Model depends heavily on Semantic Drift Detection.

The Recovery Gate asks:

```text
Can recovery proceed?
```

Semantic Drift Detection asks:

```text
Does "recovery" still mean verified recovery?
```

A recovery gate should not pass if semantic drift is detected in the recovery state.

Example:

```yaml
semantic_drift_evaluation:
  semantic_drift_status: drift_detected
  drift_codes:
    - recovery_without_verification

recovery_gate_result:
  recovery_gate_status: blocked
  recovery_allowed: false
```

---

## 24. Relationship to Human Review Boundary

Semantic drift often occurs when human review is weakened or reinterpreted.

Examples:

```text
Human review required → AI review accepted.
Human approval required → no rejection treated as approval.
Review pending → treated as approved.
Not reviewed → treated as not_required.
```

The Human Review Boundary should define the expected meaning of review states.

Semantic Drift Detection should detect when those meanings are violated.

---

## 25. Relationship to Incident Lifecycle Model

Incident lifecycle states are especially vulnerable to semantic drift.

Examples:

```text
contained ≠ alert disappeared
verified ≠ AI says it is probably safe
recovered ≠ service resumed
closed ≠ nobody is looking at it anymore
```

Semantic Drift Detection should compare lifecycle labels with required structural conditions.

Example:

```text
If incident_lifecycle.phase = closed,
then recovery, review, and trace consistency requirements should be satisfied.
```

## 27. Design Principles

### 27.1 Labels Are Claims

A governance label is a claim that must be supported by structure.

### 27.2 Meaning Must Be Preserved

The meaning of states such as `verified`, `approved`, `recovered`, and `closed` must not be weakened silently.

### 27.3 Recovery Language Requires Evidence

A system should not claim recovery without verification evidence.

### 27.4 Human Review Must Not Be Reinterpreted

AI approval must not silently replace required human review.

### 27.5 Capability Gain Can Hide Governance Loss

Improvement should not be measured only by performance.

### 27.6 Trace Preserves Meaning

Trace records are required to preserve the institutional meaning of actions.

### 27.7 Drift Must Be Escalatable

When semantic drift is detected, the system should support escalation to human or higher-level governance review.

## 29. Closing Statement

AI governance does not fail only when systems break rules.

It also fails when words lose their meaning.

When "recovered" no longer means verified recovery, governance has drifted.

When "approved" no longer means proper approval, responsibility has drifted.

When "improved" means capability increased while governance weakened, civilization has drifted.

Semantic Drift Detection exists to preserve meaning.

It is the semantic sensor of the Structural AI Tuning Layer.

```text
Same label does not guarantee same meaning.
Meaning must be checked.
Structure must remember.
```
